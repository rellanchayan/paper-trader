Overview

A calm, diversified robot — built to lose less, not to gamble more

The DTA (Diversified Trend Allocator) is an automated paper-trading strategy. Instead of chasing hot stocks, it holds a small basket of broad funds, follows one simple trend rule on each, and hides in safe T-bills when things turn down. Its goal is a smoother ride, not the biggest possible win.

The big idea in one minute

Imagine five buckets: US stocks, international stocks, government bonds, gold, and real estate. Spread your money evenly across them, and keep a permanent cash cushion in ultra-safe T-bills that earns interest.

For each bucket there's one rule: if it's trending up (above its 200-day average price), hold it. If it falls below that line, sell it and move that money to T-bills. That's basically the whole strategy.

Because it owns broad funds instead of single stocks, no one company blowing up overnight can sink it — and because it steps aside from anything in a downtrend, it sidesteps the worst of big market crashes.

✅What it's good at: staying diversified, avoiding single-stock disasters, and cutting losers automatically so a bad year stays a small dent instead of a deep hole.
⚠️What to expect: in a roaring bull market it will trail a plain S&P 500 fund — sometimes for years. That's the trade-off for a calmer ride, and it's the design working as intended. See "Honest expectations" →

Why we changed

The lesson that built this strategy

The first version of this bot chased "hot" stocks with strong recent momentum. It looked clever — until it wasn't. Here's what went wrong and why the new design is safer.

What the old bot did

It scanned a list of stocks and bought whichever were climbing fastest. The problem: the fastest climbers are usually the same kind of stock — high-flying, jumpy names that all move together. The bot quietly turned into a concentrated bet.

💥The blow-up: it ended up holding four semiconductor stocks at once (AMD, AVGO, MRVL, NVDA). When chips sold off, all four hit their stop-losses on the same day. That's not four small bets — it's one big bet wearing a disguise.

Three deeper problems

🎯 One idea only

100% "momentum." That single style suffers brutal crashes when the market suddenly reverses (2009, 2020).

🕳️ Fake stops

The bot only acts once a day. A "stop" at −8% can become −20% overnight before it ever looks. It gave false comfort.

🎲 Tuning on noise

It auto-adjusted its own settings after a handful of trades — which just fits random luck, not real skill.

The shift in thinking

Old momentum botNew DTA
Picks individual hot stocksHolds broad funds (whole markets)
Tries to beat the marketTries to survive the market with a smooth ride
Can blow up on one bad nameNo single company can sink it
Sits in 0% cash when scaredEarns ~4% in T-bills when scared
Re-tunes itself constantlyFixed rules — no second-guessing
💡The old bot still exists for comparison only (run with --momentum). The DTA is the real path forward.
How it works

The portfolio: five buckets and a cash cushion

When everything is healthy, the money is split evenly across five very different funds, with a fifth of it always kept safe in T-bills. "Different" is the point — these buckets don't all fall at the same time.

🧩The trick: these five don't usually crash together. When stocks fall, bonds or gold often hold up. Spreading across them smooths out the bumps.

The cash cushion (the "harbor")

20% is always parked in ultra-short T-bill funds — about as safe as cash, but it earns the going interest rate (~4% lately). When buckets get sold for being in a downtrend, that money sails into this harbor too.

⚓To respect the rule that no single holding can exceed 25% of the account, the harbor is spread across several T-bill funds (SGOV, BIL, USFR, TBLL, GBIL), filling one before spilling into the next. Even a near-total retreat to cash stays within the rules.
How it works

The one rule: the 200-day trend gate

Each bucket is judged by a single, time-tested question: is its price above or below its average price of the last 200 days? Above means uptrend (hold). Below means downtrend (step aside).

Illustrative. The purple line is a fund's price; the dashed line is its 200-day average. Cross clearly above → hold. Cross clearly below → sell into T-bills.

Three states

▲ ON

Price is more than 2% above the 200-day line. Clear uptrend — own it.

▼ OFF

Price is more than 2% below the line. Clear downtrend — sell, go to T-bills.

↔ HOLD

Within the 2% buffer. Do nothing new — keep yesterday's decision.

🎚️Why the 2% buffer (hysteresis)? Without it, a price wobbling right at the line would trigger buy-sell-buy-sell every day. The buffer means a bucket only changes its mind when the trend clearly changes — far less pointless trading.
📚Why 200 days? It's the most studied, most stable trend signal in investing. Decades of research show that simply stepping aside when major markets fall below their long-term average dramatically reduces the worst crashes — without needing to predict anything.
How it works

The safety net: three automatic brakes

Beyond the per-bucket trend rule, three portfolio-wide brakes turn risk down (never up) when conditions worsen. They run automatically — no human, no emotion.

Brake 1
Trend gate

Any bucket below its 200-day trend is sold into T-bills.

Brake 2
Volatility

If markets get jumpy, shrink every position proportionally.

Brake 3
Drawdown

If the account falls 15% from its peak, retreat to T-bills.

Brake 2 — the volatility brake

If the market's recent swings (20-day volatility) climb above 18% a year, the strategy scales down how much it holds in the risky buckets, parking the difference in T-bills. Calmer markets → full size. Wild markets → smaller size. It never uses this to take more risk.

Brake 3 — the drawdown brake

If the whole account ever drops 15% below its highest-ever value, the strategy rotates everything risky into T-bills and waits for trends to recover.

🛑Important distinction: this brake sells and rotates — it does not freeze trading. The only thing that fully freezes the bot is a manual kill-switch file (.HALT_TRADING) that a human creates on purpose. The robot must always be able to sell to protect itself.

The honest limit: overnight gaps

🌙Because the bot only acts once a day, it cannot stop a price that crashes overnight while markets are closed. No once-a-day system can. Its real defense isn't a magic stop button — it's owning broad funds (which can't gap on one company's bad news) plus always holding a cash cushion.

How a single day works

Every weekday morning the robot wakes up and runs the same checklist. Selling-to-protect can happen any day; buying-to-grow happens once a month. That asymmetry is on purpose: be quick to reduce risk, slow and deliberate to add it.

Check yesterday's orders

See which orders actually filled. Never assume — read the real result from the broker.

Read the account & the market

How much money, what's held, how far from the peak, how jumpy is the market.

Run the three brakes

Trend gate per bucket, volatility brake, drawdown brake → decide the target mix for today.

Protect first (every day)

Sell any bucket whose trend broke, and anything not in the plan. Move that money toward T-bills.

Grow (first run of the month only)

Top up under-weight buckets that are trending up, back toward their targets.

Place safe orders & record

Sell orders first, then buys. Limit orders only. Save a plain-English summary and a risk-adjusted report.

Sell first, then buy

1 · SELLS — exit broken trends & off-plan holdings (any day)
↓
2 · PARK CASH — settled proceeds go into the T-bill harbor
↓
3 · BUYS — top up trending buckets toward target (monthly only)
🧮Guardrails built in: only LIMIT + DAY orders, whole shares, never spend more than the settled cash, no single holding over 25%, and a hard cap on how many trades per day/week. A separate checker (constitution.py) refuses any order that breaks a rule.
🐢Low effort, low cost: because buys are monthly and the trend signal is slow, the strategy trades rarely — roughly a handful of times per fund per year. Fewer trades means fewer fees and fewer mistakes.
How it works

The optional momentum "satellite"

There's a small, optional add-on that can pick a few individual momentum stocks for extra upside — but it's switched OFF by default and stays off until the safe core has proven itself on paper.

🔒Off by default. In the config, satellite_enabled = false. It only turns on after the core clears the real-money checklist — and even then it's tightly leashed.

The leash (hard caps)

LimitValueWhy
Max share of the whole account20%The safe core always stays the majority
Max in any one stock8%No single name can do real damage
Max stocks per sector2Prevents another "all chips" cluster
Only runs when…market is risk-onNo stock-picking into a falling market
🧠Core-and-satellite is a classic professional design: a big, boring, safe core does the heavy lifting, while a small satellite reaches for a little extra return — capped so a mistake there can't wreck the whole plan.
🎓This is exactly the "blend" you chose: keep the calm core, but leave room for a small, disciplined bet on individual winners — earned, not assumed.
Reality check

Honest expectations — no hype

This matters more than any feature. The DTA is built to lose less in bad markets, not to win more in good ones. If you judge it by the wrong scoreboard, you'll quit it at exactly the wrong time.

Illustrative only — not real results. SPY (grey) often climbs higher in good times but takes deep falls. The DTA (purple) aims for a smoother line with shallower dips, accepting a lower peak.

What to realistically expect

MeasureRough expectation
Raw return vs SPYOften lower, especially in strong bull markets — possibly for years
Volatility (bumpiness)Roughly half of an all-stock portfolio
Worst-case drawdownTarget ~15–20%, versus 30–50% for stocks alone
How it "wins"Better risk-adjusted return (Sharpe / return-per-drop), not bigger raw return
Trading frequencyLow — a few trades per fund per year
📉It WILL underperform SPY in: strong bull markets, especially narrow "mega-cap melt-ups" like the current one; choppy sideways markets (the trend can whipsaw); and sharp V-shaped rebounds (it re-enters a bit late). Expect it to trail in roughly half of all years. That is the design working — not a reason to abandon it.
⚖️The right scoreboard: compare Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, and volatility against SPY — not the raw monthly return. The daily report (dta_metrics.py) prints exactly these.
🧠The #1 risk is you, not the model. The most likely way this fails is a human getting impatient during an expected stretch of underperformance and switching it off — right before it does its job in the next downturn.

Path to real money

This is paper money only today. Before a single real dollar, everything below must be true. The checklist exists because the biggest danger is rushing.

A · Code that must be proven

  • ✓
    Drawdown brake rotates, doesn't halt. A −15% event sells into T-bills; only a manual switch freezes the book. tested
  • ✓
    Daily protection works. A bucket that breaks trend is sold the very next run. tested
  • ✓
    Safe ordering. Sells before buys, never overspend cash, no holding over 25%. tested
  • ✓
    No self-tuning. The DTA's settings are frozen — no learning on noise.
  • ○
    Unfilled-order retry. Re-price a limit order that didn't fill and track the fill rate. to build
  • ○
    Missed-run catch-up. If a scheduled run is skipped, do the protective leg first next time. to build

B · Paper track record (12–24 months)

  • ○
    Live through one full cycle: a bucket going risk-off → into T-bills → and back. If the gate never fires, it's untested.
  • ○
    Sharpe ≥ SPY and return-per-drawdown ≥ SPY, with clearly lower volatility and shallower drops.
  • ○
    Worst drop stays in the ~15–20% design band.
  • ○
    Order fill rate ≥ 90%, logged weekly.

C · Guardrails before funding

  • ○
    Account ≥ ~$25–50k so whole-share rebalancing actually works.
  • ○
    A written promise not to abandon it during expected underperformance.
  • ○
    Stage the money: start with 10–25%, scale up only after a real risk-off→risk-on cycle with real fills.

Reference

Glossary — plain English

Every bit of jargon on this site, in one place.

ETF (fund)
A single ticker that holds a whole basket of investments — e.g. SPY holds all 500 big US companies. One purchase, instant diversification.
200-day average (SMA200)
The average closing price over the last ~200 trading days. A widely used line between "long-term uptrend" and "downtrend."
Trend gate
Our one rule: price above the 200-day line → hold the fund; below → sell it and move to T-bills.
Hysteresis (the 2% buffer)
A small dead-zone around the line so the strategy doesn't flip-flop when price hovers right at it.
T-bills / the harbor
Ultra-short US government debt funds (SGOV, BIL…). About as safe as cash, but earns interest. Where money hides when markets are risky.
Volatility
How much prices swing. High volatility = bumpy and risky; low = calm.
Drawdown
How far the account has fallen from its highest-ever value. A "15% drawdown" means down 15% from the peak.
Sharpe ratio
Return earned for each unit of bumpiness. Higher = a smoother, more efficient ride. The DTA's true scoreboard.
Rebalance
Topping buckets back up to their target sizes (done monthly here).
Risk-on / risk-off
Risk-on = markets healthy, own growth assets. Risk-off = markets shaky, retreat to safety.
LIMIT + DAY order
An order to trade only at a set price or better, that expires at day's end if it doesn't fill. Safer than a "buy at any price" market order.
Core & satellite
A big safe "core" portfolio plus a small, capped "satellite" reaching for extra return.
🎉That's the whole strategy. Calm, diversified, rule-based, and honest about its limits.
