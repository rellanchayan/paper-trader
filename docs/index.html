BLEND Strategy Docs
Overview

A hand-picked stock portfolio with an ETF safety anchor

The BLEND strategy puts 75% of the money into a diversified set of hand-picked individual stocks (the growth engine) and 25% into broad ETFs (the ballast). It buys slowly — never more than $50,000 a day — follows trend rules to cut losers, and is built to obey a Robinhood "agentic" investment account: no day trading, no options, no margin.

The idea in one minute

Most of the portfolio (75%) is a basket of roughly a dozen individual stocks chosen for strong, steady uptrends — but spread across sectors so it's never a bet on just one industry. The other 25% is a calm ETF anchor (broad US stocks + bonds + gold) that steadies the ride.

It only deploys up to $50,000 per day, so a single day's prices can never consume the whole account at once. It sells a stock when its trend breaks, steps aside entirely when the whole market turns down, and parks idle money in interest-earning T-bills.

Every rule is chosen to stay inside what a Robinhood agentic investment account allows — long-term-style investing, never day trading.

⚠️This is the higher-growth, higher-risk choice. Owning 75% individual stocks can grow faster than a broad fund — but individual stocks can also fall hard and gap overnight. The guardrails below reduce that risk; they don't erase it. See "Honest expectations" →
✅What keeps it sane: max 2 stocks per sector, ≤10% in any one name, a "don't pick two near-identical stocks" filter, automatic trend-break exits, and a market-wide off-switch that moves everything to safety in a downturn.
Why this design

How we got here

This strategy is the result of three deliberate steps — and one decision you made about how much risk you want.

v1 — Pure momentum bot

Chased the hottest stocks. It accidentally bought four semiconductor names at once and they all crashed together. Lesson: concentration kills.

v2 — DTA (all-ETF, ultra-safe)

An all-ETF "lose less" design. Very safe, but built to trail the market in raw return — more caution than you wanted.

v3 — BLEND (this one)

Your call: reach for more growth with 75% hand-picked stocks, but keep hard diversification limits so it can never become "all chips" again, plus a 25% ETF anchor and a daily spend cap.

🧭The honest trade-off you accepted: more individual-stock exposure means more potential upside and more potential drawdown than the all-ETF version. The guardrails are what make that risk deliberate instead of reckless.

The two safer alternatives still live in the code and can be switched on any time: --dta (all-ETF anchor) and --momentum (the original, for comparison only).

How it works

How stocks get picked

A stock only enters the 75% sleeve if it passes a series of filters — then survives the diversification gates. Held stocks are kept until their trend breaks, so the bot isn't constantly churning.

1 · Must be in a real uptrend

Price above its 50-day average, which is above its 200-day average. No catching falling knives.

2 · Must be beating the market

Its recent return has to beat the S&P 500 — relative strength, not just "up a little."

3 · Ranked by risk-adjusted momentum

A blend of 1-, 3-, and 6-month gains divided by how jumpy the stock is — rewards steady climbers over wild swingers.

4 · Diversification gates

Keep going down the ranked list, but skip a name if its sector is already full (2) or it moves almost identically to one already chosen.

5 · Sized by calmness

The ~12 survivors split the 75% budget weighted toward lower-volatility names, each capped at 10%.

🔒Double-locked. These limits are enforced in two places: the strategy plans within them, and a separate checker (constitution.py) refuses any order that would break the $50,000/day cap, the no-day-trade rule, the 25%-per-holding cap, or spend more cash than is settled.

Why a $50,000/day cap helps

Instead of dumping all your cash into the market in one order, it spreads deployment across a few days — a built-in guardrail. You're never fully exposed to a single day's prices, and a bad pick can't consume the whole account at once.

🌙Honest limit: like any once-a-day system, it can't react to an overnight crash before markets reopen. Owning ~12 diversified names (not one big bet) and capping each at 10% is what limits the damage from any single surprise.
How it works

The safety net

Three portfolio-wide brakes sit on top of the per-stock trend rule. They only ever turn risk down, automatically.

Brake 1
Market regime

If the S&P 500 is below its 200-day average, it stops picking stocks entirely.

Brake 2
Volatility

If markets get jumpy, it shrinks how much it holds in risk assets.

Brake 3
Drawdown

Down 15% from the peak → rotate everything to T-bills.

🚦The big one is the regime brake. Picking individual stocks is only allowed while the overall market is in an uptrend. The moment the market breaks down, the 75% stock engine switches off and existing stocks are sold to safety — exactly when single stocks are most dangerous.
🛑The drawdown brake sells and rotates to T-bills — it does not freeze trading. Only a manual kill-switch file (.HALT_TRADING) fully stops the bot, and a human creates that on purpose.
How it works

How a single day works

Each weekday morning it runs the same routine: protect first, then invest up to $50,000 — selling fast, buying slow.

Check fills & read the account

What actually filled yesterday, how much cash/equity, how far from the peak, how much it has already invested today.

Run the brakes

Market regime, volatility, drawdown → decide today's target mix.

Protect (uncapped)

Sell any stock whose trend broke, anything off-plan, or everything if a brake fired. Reducing risk is never throttled.

Invest (capped at $50,000)

Top up the stock sleeve toward target, then the ETF anchor, then park leftovers in T-bills — stopping at the daily cap.

Place safe orders & report

Sells first, then buys. Limit orders only. No same-day round trips. Save a summary + risk-adjusted report.

1 · SELLS — broken trends & off-plan names (uncapped)
↓
2 · BUYS — stocks → ETF anchor → T-bills, until $50,000 is reached

Reference

Glossary — plain English

Every term used on this site.

Stock vs ETF
A stock is one company (e.g. Apple). An ETF is a basket bundled into one ticker (e.g. SPY = the whole S&P 500). This strategy is 75% individual stocks, 25% ETFs.
Hand-picked / momentum
The bot "picks" stocks by rules — strong, steady uptrends that are beating the market — not by a human's hunch.
Sector cap
At most 2 stocks from the same industry, so the portfolio is never a disguised bet on one sector.
Correlation filter
Won't hold two stocks that move almost identically — that's fake diversification.
Inverse-vol weighting
Calmer (less jumpy) stocks get a bigger slice; wilder ones get less.
200-day / 50-day average
Average price over the last ~200 or ~50 trading days. Above = uptrend; below = trouble.
ETF ballast
The steadying 25%: SPY (US market) + IEF (bonds) + GLD (gold).
T-bills / harbor
Ultra-safe short-term government funds where risk-off or idle cash waits, earning interest.
Day trading
Buying and selling the same security the same day. This bot never does it — it's an investor, not a day trader.
Daily invest cap
It will not place more than $50,000 of buy orders in a single day.
Drawdown
How far the account has fallen from its highest value.
Regime brake
Stops all stock-picking when the overall market is in a downtrend.
MCP connector
The plug-in standard Robinhood uses to let an AI agent trade your agentic account.
